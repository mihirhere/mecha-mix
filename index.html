The beverage industry has an inefficiency gap. Manual mixing is inconsistent and labor-bound; high-end machines are visually impressive but mechanically clunky, or fast but indifferent to taste. Mecha-Mix closes that gap with the discipline of a real engineering system — positive-displacement metering, validated thermal management, and a sanitation architecture that satisfies food-equipment code.

The system feeds five chilled ingredients through individually-driven food-grade tubing into a CFD-redesigned V-bottom mixing chamber, where a direct-coupled stainless impeller homogenizes the drink in under a second. A normally-closed solenoid pinch valve gates the nozzle as a fail-safe against gravity drip. A 144 W Peltier module — sized from a first-principles steady-state heat balance — holds the cold chamber at 5 ± 2 °C with significant thermal headroom.

“The final prototype passed all eight performance tests and was manufactured at a total component cost of $834.” — Mecha-Mix Final Design Report, May 2026

Control runs on an Arduino Giga R1 WiFi driving a finite-state machine — IDLE → SELECT → DISPENSE → MIX → SERVE → CLEAN — with a touchscreen UI, a WiFi-hosted recipe REST API, and a hardware-level fail-safe that defaults every valve to closed during boot via 10 kΩ pull-downs.

The whole machine is held together by a welding-free aluminum T-slot frame with hex-standoff corner brackets, designed around the senior design fabrication constraint that disallows welds. Plexiglass panels and a rear electronics bay isolate fluid from electronics; modular pump-bottle-valve channels make every wetted component serviceable without tools.

01 / Cooling

Solid-state refrigeration

A 144 W Peltier TEC assembly with dual-side fans and vacuum-insulated panels. CFD-validated airflow geometry directs cold air across all five bottle bases, eliminating the stagnant-zone bypass found in the original square layout.

02 / Dispensing

Positive-displacement metering

Five 12 V peristaltic pumps deliver each ingredient through isolated PE tubing. Output is mechanically fixed per revolution — independent of viscosity, temperature, or bottle fill level. Q = Vd × n.

4Validation

Eight tests, eight passes

Every spec instrumented · Every threshold met

Every performance specification was reduced to a concrete pass/fail test with an instrumented threshold. Every test passed.

We didn't write tests that would pass. We wrote thresholds that mattered to the customer.

Dose consistency on a precision scale verified the ±0.1 mL spec across all five channels. Static leak sat the system idle for 30 minutes with the solenoid de-energized — zero drops. Cleaning efficiency was a side-by-side visual comparison against a clear-water control. The Steady-State Cooling Test tracked DS18B20 readings against a calibrated IR thermometer for two hours, and converged on 4.8 °C — directly tracking the CFD-predicted 4.5 °C minimum.
