§ 02 — Engineering Deep-Dive

Every subsystem in Mecha-Mix was sized from a governing equation before it was built. This page walks through the calculations behind the dispensing accuracy, cooling capacity, mixing torque, electrical budget, and embedded control logic.

§ 01 / Dispensing

The accuracy specification — ±0.1 mL across any viscosity, temperature, and bottle fill level — rules out time-based valves and gravity feeds. Both depend on flow rate, which depends on viscosity, head pressure, and tubing condition. A positive-displacement peristaltic pump sidesteps the whole problem: the volume per revolution is fixed by tube geometry and roller spacing, period.

Eq. 1 — Volumetric output
(1)
$$Q = V_d \cdot n$$

where Q is volumetric flow rate [mL/s], Vd is the displaced volume per revolution (a constant set by tube ID and roller geometry), and n is rotational speed. Because Vd is geometry-dependent and not pressure- or viscosity-dependent, the ±0.1 mL spec is achievable across all liquid types without a feedback sensor. Each pump channel is driven by a PWM signal from the Arduino Giga R1, so n is software-controlled and recipes execute digitally.

The dispensing test verified this experimentally — ten consecutive doses on every channel landed within the spec band, with variation imperceptible to the naked eye on a precision scale.

A compressor-based system was rejected on size, weight, noise, and refrigerant handling — all of which conflict with a countertop form factor. A Peltier TEC is solid-state, refrigerant-free, and can be bonded directly to the chassis.

Eq. 2 — Peltier cooling capacity
(2)
$$Q_c = \alpha \, T_c \, I - \tfrac{1}{2} I^2 R - K \, \Delta T$$

For the 144 W module at 12 A, the maximum achievable ΔT is approximately 65 °C. The operating ΔT is only ~15 °C (20 °C ambient to 5 °C target), so the module runs with substantial thermal headroom.

The steady-state load is dominated by conduction through the plexiglass enclosure walls, set by Fourier's law:

Eq. 3 — Fourier conduction
(3)
$$\dot{Q}_{cond} = \frac{k \cdot A \cdot \Delta T}{L}$$

With k = 0.19 W/m·K (plexiglass), A ≈ 0.35 m², L = 0.006 m, and ΔT = 15 °C:

Eq. 4 — Worked example
(4)
$$\dot{Q}_{cond} = \frac{0.19 \times 0.35 \times 15}{0.006} \approx 166 \text{ W}$$

That's right at the limit of the TEC's rating, which motivated the vacuum-insulated panel strategy — they reduce effective k by ~10×, dropping the conductive load to ~17 W. That's deep within the TEC's capacity and slashes the duty cycle.

CFD-driven geometry

The first CFD pass showed cold air bypassing the lower bottle cavities — the system was technically cold but the bottles weren't being convected. The vent placement and internal geometry were redirected toward the bottles, eliminating the stagnant zones. The redesigned chamber converges on 4.5 °C in simulation and held a measured 4.8 °C steady-state over two hours in the validation test.

The mixing problem looks simple — a motor and a stick — but the worst-case ingredient (a high-viscosity syrup at μ = 0.5 Pa·s, ρ = 1300 kg/m³) deserves verification. The Reynolds number sets the flow regime, the regime sets the Power Number, and the Power Number sets the torque.

Eq. 5 — Impeller Reynolds number
(5)
$$Re = \frac{\rho \, n \, D^2}{\mu} = \frac{1300 \times 8.33 \times (0.04)^2}{0.5} = 34.6$$

Re < 100, so flow is laminar. That gives a Power Number Kp ≈ 40, and:

Eq. 6 — Power required
(6)
$$P = K_p \cdot \mu \cdot n^2 \cdot D^3 = 40 \times 0.5 \times (8.33)^2 \times (0.04)^3 = 0.089 \text{ W}$$
Eq. 7 — Required torque
(7)
$$T = \frac{P}{\omega} = \frac{0.089}{52.36} = 1.7 \text{ N·mm}$$

The 12 V DC mixing motor is rated at ~500 N·mm. The required torque is three orders of magnitude below capacity. Massive margin.

FEA on the impeller

With the load known (1.7 N·mm moment, fixed support at the motor coupling), an ANSYS static structural simulation compared Bambu PLA against Dental Resin. Both yielded Factor of Safety > 60. PLA won on three counts: 32% less deformation than resin, lower cost, and faster iteration.

The Peltier dominates the current budget:

(8)
$$I_{Peltier} = \frac{P}{V} = \frac{144}{12} = 12 \text{ A}$$

Three passive protection networks

The 3.3 V Arduino GPIO pins are protected by three redundant networks. Gate resistors (220 Ω) limit inrush current from charging MOSFET gate capacitance:

Eq. 9 — Gate inrush current
(9)
$$I_{gate} = \frac{V_{pin}}{R_{gate}} = \frac{3.3}{220} = 15 \text{ mA}$$

under the pin's 20 mA limit. Pull-down resistors (10 kΩ) hold each MOSFET gate at GND during boot, forcing every valve to default Normally Closed (zero-flow) before firmware initializes. Flyback diodes (1N4007, 1 kV) across every motor and valve clamp the back-EMF spike when inductive loads de-energize, protecting both the MOSFETs and the MCU.

The firmware stack is distributed across eight source files covering the state machine, hardware abstraction layer, touchscreen UI rendering, and a WiFi-hosted recipe REST API. Safety-critical parameters — maximum dispense volume, timeout — are hard-coded in firmware and not exposed via the web API. The emergency stop is reachable from both the touchscreen Cancel button and a REST endpoint, returning the system to IDLE from any state.

§ 06 / Fabrication

No welds, in-house

No welding

The capstone fabrication rules disallowed welding. Most beverage-machine chasses are welded steel; the alternative — bolted aluminum extrusion — needs design discipline to stay rigid. The frame is built entirely from aluminum T-slot extrusions joined with T-nut fasteners, corner brackets, and hex standoffs: a tool-free, reconfigurable skeleton that survived the vibration stability test across 15 consecutive mixing cycles with no joint movement, no screw walk, and no cracked components.

The mixing chamber, pump housings, brackets, and nozzle were 3D-printed in Bambu PLA at the NYU Tandon MakerSpace. Panels were laser-cut plexiglass. The electronics bay is isolated at the rear of the machine; fluid lines route through the forward section. The mixing chamber rests on top of the dispenser housing without fixed attachments — pop it off, rinse it, drop it back.

If this went to production

The outer panels would transition to stamped food-grade stainless. PLA structural parts would move to injection-moulded ABS (above ~10,000 units to amortize the moulding cost). The T-slot frame would be replaced by a welded or bolted stainless chassis. The Arduino + breadboard would be replaced by a custom PCB. Pump heads would be sourced at OEM volume pricing.
